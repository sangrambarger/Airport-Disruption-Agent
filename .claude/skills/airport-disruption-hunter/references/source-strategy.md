# Source Strategy

## Purpose

Airport disruptions often appear first in local, official, regional, labour, aviation, or cargo
sources before they reach international media. Search where early signals actually appear.

## Source Buckets

### 1. Airport Authority Sources

Search official airport websites and public channels:

- Airport websites
- Airport newsroom pages
- Airport operational alerts
- Airport advisory pages
- Airport X/Twitter accounts
- Airport Facebook pages
- Airport LinkedIn posts
- Airport app/news pages where available

Look for:

- Airport closure
- Reopening
- Operational restrictions
- Evacuations
- Security alerts
- Weather notices
- Runway closures
- Cargo terminal notices
- Passenger warnings that imply airport-wide disruption

### 2. Civil Aviation Authority Sources

Search national and regional aviation authorities:

- FAA-style aviation authority pages
- CAA pages
- DGCA equivalents
- Airport safety notices
- Airspace or operational restrictions
- Aviation incident bulletins
- Regulator announcements

Look for:

- Airport restrictions
- Safety orders
- ATC disruption
- Runway restrictions
- Airport operating limitations
- Flight suspension notices

### 3. ATC, NOTAM, and Aviation Operations Sources

Search aviation operations sources where accessible:

- NOTAM-style notices
- ATC authority notices
- Air navigation service provider updates
- Airspace restriction notices
- Airport operational status feeds

Look for:

- Runway unavailable
- Aerodrome closed
- ATC/radar failure
- Navigation issue
- Airspace restriction around airport

### 4. Labour and Union Sources

Search labour sources aggressively. Many airport disruptions appear here early.

Sources:

- Union websites
- Union social pages
- Labour federations
- Strike calendars
- Industrial action bulletins
- Collective bargaining updates
- Worker association notices

Look for workers related to:

- Ground handling
- Cargo handling
- Refuelling
- Airport security
- ATC
- Customs
- Ramp operations
- Aircraft maintenance
- Baggage handling when it may affect turnaround

### 5. Cargo and Logistics Sources

Search cargo/logistics sources aggressively because they detect supply-chain impact faster than
passenger media.

Sources:

- Air cargo trade media
- Freight forwarding media
- Express parcel operators
- Cargo airline advisories
- Logistics company service alerts
- Airport cargo terminal notices
- Customs broker updates

Look for:

- Cargo acceptance suspended
- Cargo terminal closed
- Freight backlog
- Cargo flight cancellations
- Express operator disruption
- Customs cargo clearance delay
- Air freight capacity reduction

### 6. Airline Sources

Search airline operational pages and advisories:

- Airline travel alerts
- Airline cargo notices
- Flight suspension pages
- Network disruption notices
- Operational statements
- Strike impact advisories

Look for:

- Flights cancelled due to airport issue
- Cargo flights suspended
- Airport-specific service disruption
- Route suspensions linked to operational disruption

### 7. Local and Regional News

This is a competitive advantage area.

Search local city, regional, and airport-area media in local languages.

Prioritize sources near:

- Airport city
- Airport region
- Industrial/logistics zone
- National aviation hub

Look for direct local terms, not only English terms.

### 8. Weather Authority Sources

Search official meteorological services and severe weather alerts when airport operations may be
affected.

Look for:

- Airport warnings
- Visibility disruption
- Snow/ice closures
- Wind, fog, ash, storm, flooding affecting runway/airport operations

### 9. Police, Security, Emergency, and Customs Sources

Search official public-safety sources when an incident involves evacuation, suspicious package,
fire, explosion, accident, protest, or customs/security disruption.

### 10. Flight Tracking and Cancellation Trend Sources

Use flight tracking sources for supporting evidence, not as the sole decision basis.

Look for:

- Mass cancellations
- Diversions
- Ground stop indicators
- Departure/arrival suspension trends
- Cargo flight disruptions when visible

### 11. Social and Public Alert Sources

Use cautiously.

Can be useful for:

- First signal detection
- Passenger/worker eyewitness reports
- Airport authority social updates
- Local alerts

Must be verified through stronger sources before surfacing when possible.

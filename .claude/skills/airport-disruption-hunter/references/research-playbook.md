# Research Playbook: Airport Disruption

Part of a larger EventWatch research playbook set. It cross-references `../research-playbooks.md`
(shared source classes/research rules for every event type) and
`../../governance/threshold-rulebook.md` (this event type's reportability rules) — those files are
**not included in this skill pack**, so treat the pointers below as context from the source
material rather than resolvable links. This file only covers *how* to research an airport
disruption, not whether it clears any particular reportability bar.

## Research objective

Determine whether airport operations, cargo movement, flight schedules, terminal operations,
runway operations, airspace access, or logistics flows have been disrupted.

## Mandatory fact categories

- Airport name
- City, state/province, country
- Disruption cause
- Disruption start date and time
- Terminals, runways, cargo operations, passenger operations, or airspace affected
- Flight cancellations, diversions, suspensions, or delays
- Cargo/freight impact if reported
- Expected duration or recovery status
- Authority or operator confirmation

## Source priority

**Priority 1**: Airport authority/operations page · civil aviation authority · NOTAM or aviation
advisory source · airport social media/official advisory when it's the primary operational
channel.

**Priority 2**: Airlines and cargo carriers · local government or emergency management agency ·
airport cargo terminal operator · logistics providers.

**Priority 3**: Local/native-language news · aviation trade media · international news.

## Local language strategy

Use local-language research when airport advisories, local government notices, flight disruption
reports, or local coverage aren't available in English — especially important for non-English
airport authorities and regional airports.

## Operational verification strategy

Verify whether the disruption affects flights, air cargo, runways, terminals, customs clearance,
ground handling, freight movement, or access roads. Don't treat a minor passenger delay as cargo
or supply-chain disruption unless cargo or freight impact is confirmed.

## Advanced search tactics

- **Check the airport authority's own operations/status page and NOTAMs before news coverage** —
  these update faster and more precisely than wire reports for runway closures, ground stops, and
  terminal status.
- **Separately verify cargo/freight impact, not just passenger disruption** — a headline about
  flight delays doesn't tell you whether cargo operations are affected at all.
- **For a non-mapped airport, research whether it handles cargo at all** before spending more
  research time on it.
- Consider airport criticality context (which covered industries depend on this airport) early —
  it helps prioritize which operational facts to chase down first.

## Verification cross-checks

- **Distinguish "disruption is passenger-only" from "cargo unconfirmed either way"** — the latter
  needs more research, not an assumption that no cargo mention means no cargo impact.
- **For a labour-related disruption, verify whether it actually disrupts airport operations**
  (flights, cargo, ground handling) or is confined to non-operational premises — a labour dispute
  with confirmed contingency plans holding may stay classified as a labour story rather than an
  airport-operations one.

## Common pitfalls

- Treating a hangar/parking-lot fire with no flight impact as reportable — confirm actual
  flight/cargo impact before treating any airport incident as an operational disruption.
- Applying a duration-based severity distinction before actually confirming whether the airport
  handles cargo/is significant to the relevant supply chains — both dimensions matter, not
  duration alone.
- Missing that a nationwide strike may require assessing **each individual airport** affected,
  not one blanket country-wide judgment — a common shortcut that understates or overstates
  exposure depending on which airports actually matter.

## Escalation triggers

- If a disruption crosses or is expected to cross the 24-hour mark, treat that as a materially
  more significant case.
- If a strike ballot's status changes from unconfirmed to confirmed, or contingency plans that
  were holding stop holding, re-check whether the story should now be reclassified from a labour
  story to an airport-operations disruption.

## Research completion criteria

Research is complete when the airport, disruption cause, operational status, affected operations,
source confirmation, and recovery status are known or explicitly unavailable.
